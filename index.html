I am a Master's student in Statistical Science at Duke University, working with Prof. Ethan X. Fang (Duke), Prof. Junwei Lu (Harvard), and Prof. Jesús A. De Loera (UC Davis). I received my B.Sc. in Mathematics with Finance with First Class Honours from the University of Liverpool, through the 2+2 program with Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University.

My research focuses on multi-agent AI systems for scientific discovery. I build systems in which agents propose, test, and verify results, and I study how to evaluate these systems and decide when their results can be trusted. I have worked mainly on mathematics, and I hope to carry these methods into biomedical research.

Research Experience

Synthetic Conjecture Generation and Nano-LLM Training for Graph Theory
Duke University, 09/2026 to present. With Prof. Ethan X. Fang and Prof. Junwei Lu.
  • Extending a forward-chaining engine that generates graph-theory conjectures with diverse statements and proofs, each verified in Lean 4. Proved results are reused as premises for harder ones.
  • Training a nano-scale language model on this data to solve new graph-theory problems, with open problems such as Barnette's conjecture as a long-term goal.
Autonomous Formalization of Polytope Conjectures in Lean 4
Duke University, 04/2026 to 09/2026. With Prof. Jesús A. De Loera, Prof. Ethan X. Fang, and Prof. Junwei Lu.
  • Built the prover of the closed-loop system above, a pipeline that turns machine-generated conjectures on simple 3-polytopes into verified Lean 4 proofs.
  • Restricted the prover to a fixed, hand-audited inventory of foundational polyhedral facts missing from Mathlib, and decomposed each theorem into smaller lemmas that are proved and cached separately.
  • The pipeline produced four end-to-end machine-checked proofs that rely only on this inventory, with no new sorry and no human intervention after initialization.
Counterexample Search for Polytope Conjectures with Reinforcement Learning
Duke University, 01/2026 to 05/2026. With Prof. Jesús A. De Loera, Prof. Ethan X. Fang, and Prof. Junwei Lu.
  • Framed counterexample search for machine-generated conjectures on simple 3-polytopes as a sequential decision problem solved by a PPO agent.
  • Designed actions as local, face-preserving graph operations and used a graph-based policy network conditioned on the current polytope.
Graph-Based Anomaly Detection for Third-Party Risk
Duke University capstone sponsored by GSK, 09/2026 to present.
  • Building a graph-based model that flags unusual relationship patterns among about 20,000 synthetic third-party vendors.
  • Building the graph only from information available at each point in time to prevent temporal leakage, and measuring what each stage of the model adds through a staged ablation scored by the sponsor against held-out labels.
Bayesian Survival Analysis for Lung Cancer Data
University of Liverpool, final-year master's-level research project, 2025. [PDF]
  • Derived likelihoods for the Cox and parametric survival models, wrote a Metropolis-Hastings sampler in R, and compared Bayesian and frequentist estimates of the effect of age on survival in the NCCTG lung cancer data.

Online Mixture of Autoregressive Experts
ECE 685D Introduction to Deep Learning project, Duke University, 2026, in progress. [Code]
An online forecaster for vector time series that combines autoregressive experts, re-fitted at every step with the Levinson-Durbin recursion, through a gate that starts from the fixed-share rule of Ding, Zhou and Tarokh and learns a residual correction online.
A Multi-Agent System for Generating NYT Connections Puzzles
Independent project, 2026. [Code]
An agent pipeline that generates Connections-style word puzzles across four difficulty levels, combining classifiers trained on past puzzles, external knowledge sources, and LLM-based quality checks.
Predicting Earthquake-Induced Building Damage in Nepal

A comparison of gradient boosting, neural network, and regularized regression models for predicting damage levels of 260,000 buildings after the 2015 Nepal earthquake.
