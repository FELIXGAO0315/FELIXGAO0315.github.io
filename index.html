I am a Master's student in the Department of Statistical Science at Duke University, working with Prof. Ethan X. Fang (Duke), Prof. Junwei Lu (Harvard), and Prof. Jesús A. De Loera (UC Davis). I received my B.Sc. in Mathematics with Finance with First Class Honours from the University of Liverpool, through the 2+2 program with Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University.

My research focuses on multi-agent AI systems for scientific discovery. I build systems in which agents propose, test, and verify results, and I study how to evaluate these systems and decide when their results can be trusted. I have worked mainly on mathematics, and I hope to carry these methods into biomedical research.

Synthetic Conjecture Generation and Nano-LLM Training for Graph Theory
Duke University, 09/2026 to present. With Prof. Ethan X. Fang and Prof. Junwei Lu.
  • Extending a forward-chaining engine that generates graph-theory conjectures with diverse statements and proofs, each verified in Lean 4. Proved results are reused as premises for harder ones.
  • Training a nano-scale language model on this data to solve new graph-theory problems, with currently open problems as a long-term goal.
Optimal Hexagon Bounds for Simple 3-Polytopes
Duke University, 01/2026 to present. With Prof. Jesús A. De Loera, Prof. Ethan X. Fang, and Prof. Junwei Lu. Submitted to SoCG 2027.
  • Settled a long-standing open question on face vectors of simple 3-polytopes by constructing the first explicit infinite family that needs 3σ − 12 or 3σ − 14 hexagons, proving that the coefficient 3 in Fisher's bound cannot be lowered.
  • Determined the complete set of realizable hexagon counts for all 19 p-vectors with faces of at most six sides, six of them for the first time, through a uniform construction of self-reproducing local operations backed by exhaustive enumeration.
  • Developed the machine-learning component, building on the RL polytope-search framework of our counterexample project, and co-wrote and revised sections of the final paper.
Autonomous Formalization of Polytope Conjectures in Lean 4
Duke University, 04/2026 to 09/2026. With Prof. Jesús A. De Loera, Prof. Ethan X. Fang, and Prof. Junwei Lu.
  • Built the prover of the closed-loop system above, a pipeline that turns machine-generated conjectures on simple 3-polytopes into verified Lean 4 proofs.
  • Restricted the prover to a fixed, hand-audited inventory of foundational polyhedral facts missing from Mathlib, and decomposed each theorem into smaller lemmas that are proved and cached separately.
  • The pipeline proved four conjectures end to end with machine-checked proofs that rely only on this inventory, with no new sorry and no human intervention after initialization.
Counterexample Search for Polytope Conjectures with Reinforcement Learning
Duke University, 01/2026 to 05/2026. With Prof. Jesús A. De Loera, Prof. Ethan X. Fang, and Prof. Junwei Lu.
  • Framed counterexample search for machine-generated conjectures on simple 3-polytopes as a sequential decision problem solved by a PPO agent.
  • Designed actions as local, face-preserving graph operations and used a graph-based policy network conditioned on the current polytope.
Graph-Based Anomaly Detection for Third-Party Risk
Duke University capstone sponsored by GSK, 09/2026 to present.
  • Building a temporal heterogeneous graph model that detects 14 typologies of anomalous relationship patterns, such as shared addresses, sponsor-approver coupling, layered ownership, and bridge entities, among about 21,000 synthetic third-party vendor records. The graph uses only information observed by each period end, and the model returns a ranked list of review cases.
  • Built the vendor-level baseline and the relationship-feature layer. Relationship features alone tripled test AP over the vendor-level baseline.
  • Took over the graph model after the team's first version and built every later version. v2.0 was rebuilt on 43 validation cases released by the sponsor, with a period filter, cross-border corridor and sponsor-burst detectors, and chance-based down-ranking of noisy findings, and the overfitting risk of every change recorded.
  • v2.0 raised validation AP from 0.31 to 0.42. In the sponsor's blind test scoring it reached precision of 0.90 in the top 10, 0.68 in the top 25, and 0.50 in the top 50, with typology accuracy of 0.87, up from 0.50, 0.48, 0.38, and 0.79 in the first submission.

Projects

Online Mixture of Autoregressive Experts
ECE 685D Introduction to Deep Learning project, Duke University, 2026. [Code]
  • Built an online forecaster for vector time series that combines VAR experts of orders 1 to L, each re-fitted at every step from exponentially weighted autocovariances with the multichannel Levinson-Durbin recursion.
  • Designed a softmax gate that starts exactly from the fixed-share rule of Ding, Zhou, and Tarokh and learns a zero-initialized MLP residual online, with weight decay pulling it back toward the fixed rule.
  • Evaluated strictly online on synthetic regime-switching data, ETTh1, and sunspots. On ETTh1 the learned gate was the best causal method (1.03% lower cumulative MSE than the best single expert), and adding non-AR experts cut its MSE by a further 8%.
