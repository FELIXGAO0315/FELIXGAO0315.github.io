— 01 / Research

What I work on.

I'm interested in AI for specific fields — building systems that do real work inside a domain, not just answer questions about it. The domains I care about most are mathematics, biomedicine, and finance: each has its own data, its own rules for what counts as correct, and its own cost of being wrong.

Across them, I keep returning to three questions. How should agentic systems be designed so that models can propose, test, and check ideas on their own? How do language models actually learn, from pre-training through RL-based post-training? And how can structure — proofs, graphs, relationships between entities — make a model's output something an expert can trust?

A Closed-Loop System of 3 Mathematical Agents: Conjecturer, Refuter, and Prover

Jesús A. De Loera, Jillian Eddy, Ethan X. Fang, Yuan Gao, Junwei Lu (alphabetical order)

A closed-loop autonomous system for mathematical discovery in polyhedral combinatorics. After initialization, a single command runs conjecture generation, counterexample search, and formal proving in Lean 4, then feeds the outcomes into the next generation with no human intervention between stages. Proposal, verification, and learning are kept separate: feedback can steer the conjecture generator, but never the counterexample validator or the proof-acceptance gates. The campaign has produced 204 witness-verified refutations, four end-to-end machine-proved conjectures with no sorry, and three open conjectures, each kept as a reusable computational or formal artifact.

Synthetic Theorem Generation for Neural Theorem Proving in Lean 4

with Prof. Ethan X. Fang and Prof. Junwei Lu

  • Designing an AlphaGeometry-inspired engine that generates an unbounded stream of Lean 4–verified theorems in 3-polytope combinatorics and graph theory as training data for LLM theorem provers.
  • Built a forward-chaining deduction engine (linear combination, integer rounding, case splitting) over a closed axiom base; every theorem is checked by the Lean 4 kernel, so all data is true and machine-proved.
  • Proved theorems are reused as premises for later ones, so difficulty compounds; each theorem is scored for interestingness and proof-length hardness to build training curricula.
  • Extending the axiom base to general graph theory on Mathlib's SimpleGraph and packaging the generator as an open Lean RL environment for proving agents.

Autonomous Formalization of Polytope Conjectures in Lean 4

with Prof. Jesús A. De Loera, Prof. Ethan X. Fang, and Prof. Junwei Lu

  • Architected the theorem-proving arm of the closed-loop system above: a nine-stage pipeline that turns machine-generated conjectures on simple 3-polytopes into verified Lean 4 proofs.
  • Decomposed each theorem into a dependency-aware proof DAG of typed lemma nodes, proved in parallel batches with content-hashed caching to stay stable across non-deterministic LLM runs.
  • Built a closed axiom base and a growing proof library for polyhedral results missing from Mathlib (Euler's formula, Barnette's bound), eliminating hallucinated lemma names.
  • Delivered end-to-end machine-verified proofs with zero sorry and no human in the loop.

AI-Driven Counterexample Discovery for Polytope Conjectures

  • Framed counterexample search for machine-generated (TxGraffiti) conjectures on simple 3-polytopes as a sequential decision process solved by a PPO agent — the search arm of the same closed-loop system.
  • Designed the action space as local face-preserving graph operations (e.g., Y→Δ insertion) under face-type constraints, with soft-constraint reward shaping toward valid extremal constructions.
  • Implemented a graph-based policy network with FiLM conditioning on the current polytope's structural features; verified counterexamples fed back into the Lean 4 pipeline.

A Multi-Agent System for Generating NYT Connections Puzzles

An end-to-end agentic pipeline that generates thousands of NYT Connections-style word puzzles at scale, spanning all four difficulty tiers (Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple). The system fuses three layers: ML classifiers and rule-based gates trained on the real NYT corpus to assign difficulty, external knowledge sources (WordNet, Wikidata, Datamuse) for seed material, and selective LLM judging for quality control. The current release introduces a five-template puzzle assembler — Random, Misdirection, Anchor, Surface Trap, and Trojan Horse — each targeting a distinct misdirection pattern players experience. The entire LLM layer runs asynchronously in parallel, bounded only by upstream rate limits. Across a 100-puzzle benchmark, 99/100 used a non-trivial template at quality avg 8.53.

Predicting Earthquake-Induced Building Damage in Nepal

A multiclass classification study on the 2015 Nepal earthquake dataset — 200,000 training and 60,000 test buildings, each described by 38 structural, geographic, and usage features, with the goal of predicting damage severity (low / medium / high). I benchmarked five approaches under a unified preprocessing pipeline: XGBoost, CatBoost, an MLP with embedding layers, multinomial logistic regression, and ElasticNet. Tuned XGBoost with target-encoded geographic features performed best (Accuracy 0.7442, Quadratic Weighted Kappa 0.6177), confirming that hierarchical geo IDs and tree-based interactions are the primary signal in the data. The notebook documents the full EDA, hyperparameter search, and a comparative analysis of how each model trades off accuracy against ordinal coherence.

A Bayesian Treatment of Survival Analysis

An independent implementation of Bayesian methods for time-to-event analysis, developed as a deliberate counterpart to the dominant frequentist toolkit. The project works through parametric survival models under a Bayesian framework — prior specification, posterior inference via MCMC, and posterior predictive checks — and examines how Bayesian estimates handle censoring, small samples, and the incorporation of historical information. The aim is both pedagogical and applied: to make the case that for medical and clinical-trial contexts, where decisions are costly and prior evidence is real, the Bayesian framework offers genuine inferential advantages over likelihood-based alternatives.
